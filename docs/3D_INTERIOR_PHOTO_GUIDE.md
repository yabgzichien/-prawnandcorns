# Photo capture guide for the 3D interiors

Purpose: collect reference photos so we can model the inside of the A blocks, the A3 library, D6 (Canteen D6) and LY3 (the cafeteria) in the 3D campus. We model by hand from references and measurements, so what we need is clear, straight, well-labelled photos and a few measurements, not artistic shots.

Fictional data note: the 3D rooms will show demo data only. We are copying the look and layout of the places, not any real records.

## 1. Ground rules

- Ask first. Tell the library desk, the guards or the block's staff what the photos are for. Do not enter staff-only areas, locked labs or rooms where a class or exam is running.
- No people. Wait for a gap, or shoot early in the morning, at lunch or just after classes end. If someone is in frame by accident, retake it.
- No readable personal data: no student screens, ID cards, exam papers or private notices. Public signage is fine and wanted.
- Do not use flash. Switch the lights on if they are off.
- Keep every photo. Delete nothing on site, even blurry ones. We sort later.

## 2. Phone settings (all teammates use the same)

- Camera app, normal photo mode, back camera, 1x lens. Do not use the 0.5x ultra-wide lens (it bends straight walls) and do not use digital zoom.
- Turn on the grid lines and use them: horizons and door frames must be level, vertical lines must stay vertical.
- Turn off portrait mode, beauty filters, night mode if it blurs, Live Photo and any filter.
- Landscape orientation for rooms, portrait for tall things (door, stair, pillar, lift).
- Tap to focus on the main subject, hold still, take it twice if unsure.
- Hold the phone at chest or eye height, about 1.4 to 1.6 m, and keep the same height all day. Do not tilt up or down. If you must show the ceiling, take a separate ceiling photo.
- Keep roughly 30 percent overlap between neighbouring photos so we can see how they join.
- Optional bonus: if someone has an iPhone Pro or iPad Pro, a LiDAR scan with Scaniverse or Polycam of the big spaces (library floor, lecture hall, canteen) is welcome, on top of the photos, not instead.

## 3. The shot pattern for any room (about 12 to 16 photos)

1. Door shot: the door from outside, then the room as seen from the doorway.
2. Four corner shots: stand in one corner and shoot diagonally to the opposite corner. Repeat from each of the four corners. Each shot shows two walls and the floor.
3. One straight-on photo of each wall, as square as possible, with the whole wall in frame.
4. Ceiling: one photo pointing straight up, showing the light layout, air-con units and ceiling tile grid.
5. Floor: one photo looking down at the floor so the tile or carpet pattern and its size are visible.
6. Close-ups of things we will model: door and handle, window and frame, light fixture, whiteboard or screen, podium or desk, one chair and one table, power sockets, air-con unit, any step or ramp.
7. Scale reference: one photo of a wall or table with a tape measure laid on it. If you have no tape, use a sheet of A4 paper (29.7 cm by 21 cm) flat against the wall.

Also write down (in the shot log, section 7): room length, width and ceiling height (a laser measure, the phone Measure app, or count floor tiles and note the tile size), door width, number of seats, number of rows.

## 4. Corridors, lobbies, stairs, lifts, atria

- Corridors: stand at each end and shoot straight down the length. Then a shot at every junction and every turn. Close-up of a room door with its number sign.
- Lobby and lift area: lift doors straight on, call buttons, the lobby as a whole from two opposite corners.
- Stairs: from the bottom looking up, from the top looking down, one shot of the landing, one of the handrail and step edge. Count the steps in one flight and note it.
- Atria and open voids (A3 especially): from the edge of each floor looking across, and a photo looking up and down the void. Note which floors the void spans.
- Floor plans and evacuation maps: every floor has a fire evacuation map near the lifts or stairs. Photograph each one straight on, filling the frame, no glare, so the text is readable. These are the most valuable photos. Take them first on every floor.
- Directory boards and room number signs: photograph each one.

## 5. Exterior (needed to match the interior to the outside)

- Each block, all four sides, from far back and level (across the road, from the lake side), so the whole facade is in one frame without leaning walls.
- Entrance close-up: doors, canopy, steps, the ground floor pillars.
- One photo of a typical window row and one of the roof edge or top floor line, if visible from the ground.

## 6. What to photograph, place by place

Priority 1 means we cannot build the interior without it. Priority 2 is for polish.

### A3 including the library (Priority 1, give it the most time)

A3 has 7 floors in our data: lecture halls G01 and G02 on the ground floor, classroom 201 on Level 2, and the library on Levels 2 and 3.

- Ground floor: main entrance and lobby, lift and stair lobby, evacuation map, lecture halls G01 and G02 (full room shot pattern, plus tiered seating from the back and from the front, aisles, stage and podium, projector screen, the entrance doors).
- Every floor: the evacuation map, the lift lobby, one corridor view each way.
- Library Level 2 (learning commons): entrance gate and counter, circulation or help counter, shelving area (aisle width, shelf height, shelf end signs), open study tables, computer area, printers, the central space or void, and each wing: North wing (silent study rooms N201 to N214), South wing (open-fronted study rooms S221 to S234), East wing (group rooms E231 to E236), West wing (group rooms W241 to W246).
- For the wings do not photograph all 43 rooms. Photograph the corridor of each wing, the sign style, and these samples in full: one silent room (for example N201), one open-front study room (S221), one group room (E231), and the one at each end of the row.
- Library Level 3: the whole floor from two opposite corners, the Student Success rooms SSR1, SSR2, SSR3 (SSR3 is the larger room for 5 to 10 people), reading areas, shelves, the stairs and lift lobby.
- Classroom 201 on Level 2: full room shot pattern.
- Typical classroom floor: if the upper floors repeat, shoot one fully and, for the others, only the evacuation map, the lift lobby and anything that looks different.

### A1, A2, A4, A5 (Priority 1 for one typical floor, Priority 2 for the rest)

For each block: the ground floor lobby, one fully shot typical floor, the stair and lift areas, every evacuation map, and the named rooms below.

- A1: classrooms A1-102 and A1-210.
- A2: classrooms A2-104, A2-203 and seminar room A2-305.
- A4: classrooms A4-105 and A4-301, group study room A4-S12 (the Coding Guild hack-day uses A4-301, so shoot it well).
- A5: Computing Lab 1 and 2 (A5-L1, A5-L2) on Level 1, Electronics Lab 3 (A5-L3) on Level 2. Photograph lab benches, computer layout, cabling, ceiling services and the door. Do not touch or photograph equipment screens up close.
- If two classrooms look identical, shoot the first fully and the second with only the door, corner and scale-reference shots.

### D6 (Canteen D6, Priority 1)

Two floors in our data. Whole hall from every corner, serving counters or stalls (straight on, then angled), cashier points, the seating layout (table size, bench or chair type, spacing), the columns, ceiling and fans, the entrances, the tray return, bins and drink stations, the stair or lift if there is an upper floor, the view out of the windows. Shoot it at an empty time (between meals), plus one busy-time photo only if it shows no faces.

### LY3 (cafeteria, Priority 1)

Two floors in our data, with an open pillared ground floor. The pillar grid from a corner and from the middle, how the open sides meet the outdoors, counters and stalls, seating, the stair to the upper floor, the upper floor layout, ceiling and roof underside, the terracotta hip roof from outside and from below. Note the pillar spacing (count your paces or tiles).

### Priority 2 extras

- Lakefront Café (interior, counter and seating).
- B1 Hall and Sports Complex (entrance, main hall from one end, court markings). Only if the team has time.

## 7. Shot log and file names

Every teammate keeps a simple log (a notes page or spreadsheet) with one row per room or space:

```
Building, Level, Room or space, Date and time, Photographer, Lens used, Shot count, Measurements, Notes (what looks unusual)
```

Also draw a rough floor plan on paper or on a printed map, mark where you stood for each shot and which direction you faced. A photo of that sketch is very useful.

File names (rename after shooting, keep the original too):

```
A3_L2_NorthWing_N201_corner-NE_01.jpg
A3_G_LectureHallG01_wall-stage_01.jpg
D6_G_Hall_corner-SW_01.jpg
LY3_G_Pillars_midpoint_01.jpg
```

Pattern: Building, Level, Room or area, shot type, number. Put photos for one building in one folder, with one sub-folder per level. Upload to the team drive the same day.

## 8. Places that do not need photos

- Staff offices and departmental offices.
- Toilets: one photo of a standard toilet block (sinks, cubicle doors, tiles) is enough for all.
- Plant rooms, server rooms, store rooms, janitor closets and anything locked.
- Hostel rooms and the residence towers. LY1, LY2, LY4 to LY9 and D1 to D5 are residences, which are private. Only the LY3 cafeteria is in scope.
- Identical repeated floors and rooms: shoot one fully, then only the differences.
- Roof, parking, outdoor courts, track and field, the statue and the gate. These are outdoor and already modelled from the map.
- Library bookshelves content: no need to show book spines, only shelf height, depth, spacing and end-of-row signs.

## 9. Suggested split

- Teammate 1: A3 library Level 2 (all four wings).
- Teammate 2: A3 library Level 3, A3 ground floor lobby and lecture halls, A3 classroom 201.
- Teammate 3: A1 and A2.
- Teammate 4: A4 and A5.
- Teammate 5: D6 and LY3 (go at an off-peak meal time), then the exteriors of all blocks.

If the team is smaller, do in this order: A3 library and floor plans, D6, LY3, A3 lecture halls, one typical floor in each of A1, A2, A4, A5, then exteriors.

## 10. Quick checklist before leaving a place

- Every floor's evacuation map photographed and readable.
- Four corner shots and four wall shots of each key room.
- Ceiling and floor photos.
- At least one scale reference per room and measurements written in the log.
- Stairs, lifts and corridors covered.
- Exterior of the block taken from all four sides.
- No people or personal data visible.
- Photos renamed, backed up and logged.
